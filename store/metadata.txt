--- Description (4,000 char max) ---
You shipped a desktop site. Your phone won't show it to you.

Mobile Safari's "Request Desktop Site" only swaps the user agent — your site still lays out at phone width. The desktop CSS you wrote? Never evaluates. The breakpoints you tuned? Bypassed. Whatever your design system does at ≥768px? Invisible from your phone.

Desktop Browser actually renders pages at a true desktop viewport (1280pt by default, adjustable from 320 to 10000), scales them to fit your phone, and serves a real desktop Safari user-agent. Same engine as Safari (WKWebView). The page sees a desktop. You see the page.

WHO IT'S FOR
• Web developers who want to preview their own site at desktop breakpoints from their phone
• Anyone who's hit "this feature is hidden on mobile" — Merge buttons on GitHub PRs, deploy logs on Vercel, transaction details on Stripe, admin panels on Notion, settings deep in Linear, dashboards on AWS, the half of the UI a SaaS hides behind their mobile fallback
• Reading full desktop articles without the dumbed-down mobile layout, ads-first reflow, or "open in app" interstitial
• Capturing full-page screenshots — the entire scrollable canvas, not just the viewport
• Anyone who has ever thought "I just want the actual website"

FEATURES
• True desktop layout — CSS breakpoints evaluate against a desktop viewport, not your phone
• Adjustable resolution — pick a preset (1024, 1280, 1440, 1680, 1920) or set any custom width up to 10000
• One-tap full-page screenshot — capture the entire scrollable page and share via Messages, Mail, AirDrop, or save to Photos
• Long-press back/forward for full navigation history
• Safari-style URL bar with tap-to-select-all
• Light and dark mode, portrait and landscape, edge-swipe back/forward

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
No accounts. No ads. No tracking. No analytics. No servers. All browsing happens locally via WKWebView — same engine as Safari.

--- Notes (4,000 char max) ---
Desktop Browser renders web pages at a true desktop viewport (1280pt by default) and scales them to fit the device. To test:

1. Launch the app — it loads google.com at desktop layout.
2. Tap the URL bar, enter any URL (try wikipedia.org or apple.com) — page renders at desktop resolution, not mobile.
3. Tap the "1,280" pill in the toolbar to change viewport width (presets 1024–1920 or custom up to 10000).
4. Tap the camera icon to capture a full-page screenshot and share via the iOS share sheet.
5. Long-press back/forward arrows to see navigation history.

The app uses only WKWebView for browsing. No accounts, no tracking, no network calls beyond the user-initiated page loads.
